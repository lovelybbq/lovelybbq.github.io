Privacy Policy

Effective 20 November 2026 · Hue House for iPhone, version 1.0

The short version

The game
Keeps your progress on your phone and needs no account. It sends us anonymous play statistics, which you can turn off in Settings.
The ads
Come from Google, which collects some data about your device and the ads you see in order to show them. They use your advertising identifier only if you allow it.

What the game keeps on your phone

To remember your game between visits, Hue House saves a few things in its own storage on your phone:

  • Your progress: the level you are on, your stars, your coins, the paint in each client’s house, and the skins you have bought and wear.
  • Your settings: your choices in Settings, such as sound, music, vibration, light or dark look, graphics, frame rate and color symbols.
  • A few levels made for you: later levels are made on your phone as you play, and the last few are kept so they don’t have to be made again.

This is used only to run the game, and we cannot see it. Like the rest of your phone, it goes into your iPhone’s iCloud or computer backups, which Apple or your computer keeps, not us, so a new iPhone set up from your old one keeps your game.

If you are signed in to iCloud, the game also keeps a copy of this in your own iCloud, so it picks up where you left off if you delete it and install it again, or on another iPhone with the same Apple Account. Apple keeps this copy for your account, not us, and we cannot see it.

What the game looks at, but doesn’t keep

To pick graphics that suit your phone, the game checks whether Low Power Mode and Reduce Motion are on, roughly how new your phone is and how much memory it has, and how fast its screen refreshes. It uses this on the spot. It doesn’t store it or send it anywhere.

The only permission the game may ask for is Apple’s tracking permission, for the ads (see below). It doesn’t use your camera, microphone, photos, contacts or precise location, and it sends no notifications. Its sounds and music are files inside the app.

Ads by Google

Hue House is free and shows ads from Google AdMob: now and then an ad between levels, and ads you choose to watch for an extra undo, hint or more moves. To choose, show and count these ads, and to find problems in them, Google’s ad software in the game collects:

  • Identifiers: your phone’s advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking, and other identifiers that are limited to this app or its developer.
  • Approximate location, worked out from your IP address. Never your precise location.
  • How you use the ads and the app: the ads you see, taps, video views and app launches.
  • Diagnostics: crash logs and performance data, such as how long the app takes to start.

Google uses this to show and measure ads and for analytics, and may share it with other companies that show ads. It handles this data under its own policies: how Google uses information from apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy. Google may handle it outside your country, including in the United States, under the safeguards those policies describe. We only see totals from Google, such as how many ads were shown, never data about you.

Ads are limited to ones suitable for all ages. Ads need the internet; the game doesn’t.

Your choices about ads

  • Tracking: the game may ask once, with Apple’s prompt, whether it may track you. If you choose “Ask App Not to Track”, Google gets no advertising identifier and the ads are less tailored to you. You see the same number of ads either way. To change your answer, go to your iPhone’s Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.
  • Consent: in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent form asks you first what the ads may do. To change your choices, open Settings › Ad privacy choices in the game.
  • Ads for help are optional: you never have to watch one. Restarting a level is always free.

What we collect ourselves: anonymous play statistics

Hue House has no account or sign-in and asks for no personal information. To see how far players get and where the game is too hard, it sends us anonymous play statistics: which levels are started, won and lost, in how many moves and how long, which boosters are used, rewarded ads watched, rooms painted, taps on No ads and the coffees, and when the app is opened and closed. With them go your phone’s model, its iOS version, the game’s version and language, and a random number the game makes up when it is first opened, so the visits of one phone can be counted together. Nothing in them says who you are: no name, no account, no advertising identifier, no location.

They go to PostHog, a product analytics service, and are kept in its European Union cloud. PostHog throws away the internet address they come from. We use them only to make the game better, which is the legitimate interest we send them for; we don’t sell them or use them for ads. They are kept for a year and then deleted. You can turn them off at any time in Settings › Share play statistics, and the game then sends none.

If the game runs into an error, it may offer to send us a report. The report opens in your own mail app, written out for you to read: the error, the game’s version and language, your phone’s model and iOS version, the level you were on and the time. Nothing is sent unless you send it yourself.

Apart from these and Google’s ads, the game contains no crash-reporting or tracking tools. We don’t sell anything about you.

Apple

You get Hue House from the App Store, which is run by Apple under Apple’s Privacy Policy. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may show us crash reports and overall usage numbers that don’t identify you. We don’t receive anything else.

You can buy No ads, which ends the ads between levels, and coffees, tips for the developer that unlock nothing. Apple takes the payment through the App Store; we never see your card or your Apple Account. The game only asks the App Store whether you own No ads, each time it starts, and keeps nothing about your purchases itself. Apple sends us sales totals, not who bought what.

Deleting your data

Delete the app, and everything it kept on your phone is deleted with it, including the choices you made about ads. A backup of your phone made before still holds them until it is replaced; backups are yours, and we can’t reach them.

To start the game over without deleting it, open Settings › Reset progress. That clears your levels, coins and painted houses, and keeps your settings and skins.

The play statistics carry no name or contact details, so we cannot tell which of them are yours, and there is nothing we could delete, correct or send you by your name. Turning off Settings › Share play statistics stops them; deleting the app deletes the random number they are counted by. For data Google collected through the ads, see the Google Privacy Policy linked above. You are welcome to ask us anything about it.

Who is responsible, and your rights

Hue House is made by Denys Puziak, who is responsible for the data this policy describes and answers at huehouse.game@icloud.com. Google and PostHog, which handle data for the game, are bound by their terms to protect it at least as well as this policy describes.

Wherever you live, you can ask us what we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it. As the play statistics carry no name, we may not be able to tell which are yours. In the European Union, the UK and Switzerland, you can also object to how your data is used and complain to your data protection authority.

Children’s privacy

Hue House is made for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country). The game itself collects no personal information from anyone, its ads are limited to ones suitable for all ages, and nobody can sign up, chat, or share anything in it.

This website

These pages use no cookies or analytics, and load nothing from other sites. They open in your browser’s language, and a small script remembers the language you pick in your browser itself; it sends nothing anywhere. The company that hosts them may keep ordinary server logs, such as IP addresses, for security, under its own privacy policy.

Changes to this policy

If a future update changes what the game or its ads collect, we will update this page before that update comes out and change the date at the top. The update’s notes in the App Store will say so, and where the law or Apple requires your permission, the game will ask for it first.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or about anything else in the game: write to huehouse.game@icloud.com.
